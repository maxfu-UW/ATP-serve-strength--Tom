Method

Each surface is clustered on its own, since a strong number on grass is ordinary on clay. The six stats are 1st serve in, 1st and 2nd serve points won, service games won, aces per match and double faults per match. Each is clipped at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles so small-sample outliers can't claim a cluster, then standardized. Their average, with double faults flipped, is the strength score, and k-means with k = 3 (k-means++ seeding, best of 50 runs) splits that score into High, Mid and Low. Tiers are relative: they rank players against the others on that surface's leaderboard. ATP's own serve rating is not used for clustering because it is built from the same stats; it is shown as a check. Serve speed is not published on the leaderboard, so it isn't included.

Tournament odds use main-draw singles results from the ATP Tour results archive over the same 52 weeks, leaving out round-robin events (ATP Finals, Next Gen Finals) and walkovers. A tier's chance of winning in a round is its record in that round, pulled toward 50% by the weight of 10 matches so thin rounds don't give extreme values. Those chances are the chain's transition probabilities, and the chance of reaching each later round comes from the chain's fundamental matrix. Intervals come from 500 resamples of whole tournaments. On the all-surfaces view players are grouped by their all-surface tier; on a surface, by their tier there, or by their all-surface tier if they aren't on that surface's leaderboard. Unrated players aren't on any serve leaderboard, usually because they played too few tour matches. Tiers and results come from the same 52 weeks, so the odds show how the tiers line up with results rather than forecasting future events. The ATP archive has no results for 's-Hertogenbosch 2026, so that event is left out, and on grass the round of 128 rests on a single event, Wimbledon.
